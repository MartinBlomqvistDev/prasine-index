### Assessment
The claim is a company-wide net-zero and Paris-alignment pledge. It was therefore assessed as a pledge, and the integrity records were counted.

**Records that favour the claim.**
- The SBTi register entry (record 10, 2024) lists near-term targets validated for 2031 with a 1.5°C classification. The target is a 27% cut in Well-to-Wake scope 1 and 3 jet fuel emissions per RTK by 2031 from a 2023 base year. The register shows no net-zero target, so the validation does not extend to the 2050 goal.
- The Transition Pathway Initiative assessment (record 12, 2025) gives Ryanair Management Quality Level 5, the highest level. It gives carbon performance of 1.5 Degrees for 2030 and 2035 and Below 2 Degrees for 2050. TPI notes that it recalculated the carbon intensity under its own methodology because the company's disclosed measure is inconsistent with it.
- The linked FY26 Sustainability Statement (record 11) says the pathway "shows that decarbonisation and alignment with EU and International climate targets are possible" (PDF page 25). It says SBTi validated the near-term intensity target as aligned with a 1.5C trajectory (PDF page 16).

**Record that weighs against the claim.** LobbyMap (record 9, 2026) gives Ryanair performance band D, with engagement intensity 46 of 100, which LobbyMap describes as highly active or strategic engagement. The judge treated this as the one integrity record contradicting the pledge.

**Context on the trajectory, which the judge did not count as contradicting the claim.**
- EU ETS verified emissions for the main operator account (record 3) rose from 9,351,518 tCO2e in 2022 to 12,377,125 tCO2e in 2025, up 32%.
