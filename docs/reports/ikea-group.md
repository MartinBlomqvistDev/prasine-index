### Assessment
The claim says the policy refers to parts of the "Net Zero and Beyond" agenda, and that the agenda addresses climate impacts of the company's own operations and value chain in line with the Paris Agreement. This assessment treats it as a company-wide climate pledge. The company's wording does appear on the policy page (PDF page 36 of the annual report) [11]. The judge read no record as contradicting the claim. The company's own annual report qualifies it in the ways set out below.

Facts that favour the claim: the SBTi register entry for IKEA, updated 2024-07-11, lists near-term targets validated for FY2030 with a 1.5°C classification, and a validated net-zero target for FY2050 [10]. The near-term targets include a 50% absolute cut in scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions and an 82.1% cut in scope 1 and 2 emissions, both from an FY2016 base year [10]. LobbyMap lists IKEA at band B, which the judge counted in favour of the pledge [9]; its IKEA profile does not separate Inter IKEA Group from Ingka Group.

Qualifications from the company's own FY26 annual report [11]:
- The company says it is "currently transitioning from our previous GHG emission reduction targets to a new set of targets covering scope 1, scope 2 and material scope 3 emissions". The new targets are "planned to be ready for internal approval in FY27 and subsequently submitted to SBTi for validation" (PDF page 38).
- The company says "FY16 will therefore no longer serve as the baseline" (PDF page 38). The SBTi validation in [10] therefore concerns targets that the company says it is replacing.
- The company states: "We have not yet established a formal transition plan for climate change mitigation and are working to adopt one" (PDF page 35).
- The achieved and expected emission reduction by decarbonisation lever is not yet available (PDF page 37). Actions to 2030 are listed as medium-term actions and carry no quantified targets.
- The policy page (PDF page 36) lists three target areas but gives no figures, baseline or years.
- The pages read do not describe the Net Zero and Beyond agenda itself and do not show that the agenda or policy is in line with the Paris Agreement. They also show no independent assurance.

Reported emissions [11]: FY26 scope 1 and 2 market-based emissions were 39,429 tCO2eq, up 5%. The company says the increase in scope 1 emissions "was primarily driven by a lower use of biomass for heating of our manufacturing sites, resulting in an increase in natural gas use" (PDF page 41). Total significant scope 3 emissions were 22,220,337 tCO2eq, down 8% from 24,218,300.

EU ETS records for four IKEA Industry installations show mixed trends [2][3][4][5]. For example, the Lithuanian installation reported 14,226 tCO2e in 2023 and 15,369 tCO2e in 2025 [2]. These records cover only part of scope 1 and do not give the group's FY2016 baseline, so they cannot test progress against the SBTi targets. The E-PRTR figure of 109,000 t CO2 for one Polish facility in 2024 includes CO2 from burning biomass, so it is not comparable with the company's scope 1 figure [1].
